### The bell curve idea

When a market is balanced, most trade happens near a central price and less happens further away. Plot that sideways and you get a bell. Statistics names the two ways a real distribution misses the ideal, and both show up in profiles:

- **Skew.** The bell leans to one side. On a profile that is a fat top with a thin stem below (a P shape) or a fat bottom with a thin stem above (a b shape).
- **Kurtosis.** The peak is too sharp or the tails are too heavy. On a profile that is one dominant node, or long thin tails with almost no trade.

### See gap, fill gap

Yesterday's profile shows where trade was thin. A common rule: the next day tends to range in the place that got the least volume the day before. The same applies to a gap in the delta profile.

### Where this fights the textbook

Some order flow traders put it the other way round: price slips through areas already built with high volume and consolidates in areas of low volume. The usual teaching is the opposite: price stalls at high-volume nodes and travels fast through thin ones. Both describe something real, at different moments.

| Moment | Thin area (LVN) | Fat area (HVN) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **During an impulse** | Crossed fast. This is how it became thin. | Slows price. Holders defend, and the POC is reactive. |
| **Later, in balance** | Revisited and built up. The gap gets filled. | Already built. Little new business, so price can pass through on its way to the next gap. |

The case library shows the first row. On Nov 5 price fell through the thin zone the Nov 4 pump had left, and stopped at the fat 75.0k to 75.2k node. They do not yet show the second row, because none of the case charts follow a gap for the days after it formed.

> **Open question to test.**
>
> When price returns to a fat node, does it stall or slip? Tag journal trades with which one happened. Until you have a count, treat the POC of a fat node as reactive and a thin area as a target, not an entry.

### How to use it

- Mark the thin areas and gaps in the last few sessions. Those are where price is likely to be drawn.
- Use them as targets and as places to watch. The entry still needs a trigger from price and flow.
- A gap filled on a weekend or in a quiet session is weaker evidence than one filled with real participation.
