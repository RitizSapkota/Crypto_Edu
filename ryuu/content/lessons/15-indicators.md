### The position

- **A tool is not a strategy.** Profiles, indicators, footprint and terminals all lack the part that acts: discretion and execution.
- **A better tool without risk control only prolongs the pain.** You hit more accurately and still lose.
- **Indicators are not a sham.** Statistics work, and flow gives you timing. The mistake is reading them in isolation.
- **Avoid over-engineering.** A plain exponential moving average has decades of use behind it. A cleverer formula is rarely the missing piece.
- **Know how the crowd uses a tool.** Many traders enter on a MACD crossover. A claim worth testing: the first two candles after a cross often go the other way, which is what hurts leveraged traders who chase it.

The order in this course is price first and indicators last. Every reading below is evidence for or against a price read, never a reason to trade on its own.

### Reading any oscillator

- **Look for the anomaly.** Run two oscillators that usually agree, such as RSI and a volume oscillator. When one hits an extreme and the other barely moves, the move is suspect. Anomalies produce the best opportunities.
- **An extreme is not a signal.** Oscillators can sit at an extreme for a long time in a trend. Wait for the turn. Do not predict it.
- **Failed signals are information.** Three or four reversal signals close together that all fail mean the move is being absorbed and the original direction is likely to continue.
- **Read on the close.** Most indicator values only fix when the bar closes. An intrabar reading can vanish.

### RSI

- **Higher timeframes:** the 50 line is a trend filter. Above it, look for longs. Below it, look for shorts.
- **Lower timeframes:** divergences are useful for scalps, especially bullish divergence after a flush.
- **What it is not:** buy at 30, sell at 70. That use is why so many people think RSI does not work.

### Moving averages and trend colouring

- **Hull moving average.** Fast and smooth. A common pairing is a short one for entries and a long one as the trend line. If the short one turns but the long one holds, the trend usually stays intact unless the long one flattens.
- **Ribbon width.** A tight ribbon means a reversal is more likely. A wide ribbon means a strong trend.
- **The catch.** Fast averages overshoot, and crypto overshoots too. In chop, a trend-following average exits at break-even more often than not.
- **Candle colouring systems.** Many paid tools colour candles by bias and mark structural breaks. Treat a signal candle as a small support and resistance zone: a close beyond its far edge confirms it, a close back through its near edge invalidates it. Stops go beyond the signal candle's extreme, not on its open or close.

### Exhaustion levels

Some indicators draw support and resistance from exhaustion bars: a run of closes in one direction that stops. The logic is the tape lesson in another form. Market orders push price, run out of dry powder, volume falls, price stalls, and a level appears. Trade the rejection or bounce from it, and do not exit on the very next print, or you will round-trip most of the time.

> **What indicator marketing leaves out.**
>
> Commercial indicator guides tend to describe high accuracy. Few give settings, a sample, a win rate or any risk rules. Treat every signal as a hypothesis until your journal says otherwise.

### Check yourself

<details>
<summary>An oscillator prints three oversold readings in a row during a flush. When is the entry?</summary>

Not on the readings themselves. Wait for the first bar after they stop printing, and want a second reason from price, such as a reclaim of the swept level.

</details>

<details>
<summary>RSI makes a new extreme high, but a volume oscillator that normally tracks it barely moves. What does that suggest?</summary>

The move lacks participation. That is an anomaly, and it argues for caution on longs or for a fade, once price confirms by failing to hold the high.

</details>
