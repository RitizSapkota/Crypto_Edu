### Ranges on ranges

In a decline, each new range forms below the last one. In a rally, on top of it. A chart that looks like a confusing trend is a staircase of small ranges joined by short impulses.

### The mechanical trade

After an impulse leaves a range, price tends to come back to the edge of the range it just left. In a decline:

1. Price ranges, then impulses down into a new, lower range.
2. It comes back up to the previous range low, which now acts as resistance. That is the sell.
3. The buy-back is at the lows of the current range, or one average candle away from the previous low. If the average candle on that timeframe is 10 ticks, place the order 10 ticks away.

Mirror it for a rally: bid the previous range high, offer one average candle beyond.

Two more rules:

- **One life, one trade.** The reaction to trust is the *first* test of a level. Traders who use this treat the first reaction as close to a promise, unless abnormal pressure arrives. A second and third test of the same level is a different trade with worse odds.
- **Ranges form in two or three rotations.** They are easy to miss on time candles and clearer on profile or range-bar charts, which is one reason to use them.

> **The claim, and a caution.**
>
> Fans of this trade call it capturing the spread almost every time, and free money. It is not free. It fails when the impulse does not come back to retest, when the old range is reclaimed, and when fees and slippage eat a small target. It is a clean, testable setup. Log it as its own tag and count it.

### The same idea elsewhere

- **Dalton:** balance, imbalance, balance. The retest of the old range edge is the market checking whether the breakout is accepted.
- **Wyckoff:** accumulation, mark-up, distribution, mark-down. The same staircase on a larger scale.
- **Swing failure and break-of-structure retests** are this trade with fib levels added.

### Thin legs get revisited

A classic volume-profile teaching slide breaks a price surge into a stable base with volume support, then a thin leg with a weak base, then legs with varying volume. Its point is that efficiency tends to be restored later: the thin leg is where price comes back to. That is the see gap, fill gap rule from the profile lessons applied to a single move.

<summary>Price breaks down from a range and immediately keeps falling without a retest. What does the setup say to do?</summary>

Nothing. The entry is the retest of the old range low. No retest means no trade. Chasing the impulse is a different trade with a worse location.
