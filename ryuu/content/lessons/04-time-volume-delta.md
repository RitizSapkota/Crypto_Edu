### Presence versus participation

Time shows presence. Volume shows participation. There is no fixed rulebook for the differences, so treat the readings below as working interpretations.

| You see | Likely meaning (inferred) |
| --- | --- |
| A lot of time, little volume | Price sat there but few people committed. A holding pattern with weak sponsorship. Easy to leave. |
| A lot of volume, little time | Heavy trade in a short burst. Liquidations, stops, or large passive orders absorbing a push. |
| A lot of both | Real acceptance. A level that will matter on a return. |
| Little of both | A gap. See the previous two lessons. |

The note on that chart: the move up through the box was thin and came on a weekend with no institutional sponsorship. No meaningful short positions were trapped by it. What it did leave was net positive delta, meaning buyers. When price reverses against them, those buyers exit and the thin area retraces.

### The delta profile

Delta at a price is market buys minus market sells. A row showing minus 6.1M means sells outweighed buys by 6.1M at that price.

- Heavy negative delta at a price that holds is passive buyers absorbing sellers.
- Heavy positive delta at a price that fails is buyers trapped at the high.
- Delta only tells you who was aggressive. The price response tells you who won.

### Who pukes if it breaks

When a lot of time and volume builds at the upper side of a range, the working assumption is that sellers are being filled there. The risk is that it can always break up, and then everyone who sold into that volume has to cover. That is the same mechanism as the Nov 4 wall break in the case library: six hours of trade under the asks, then $106M of short liquidations once price got through.
