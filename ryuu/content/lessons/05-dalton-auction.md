### What a market is for

A market exists to get trade done. It moves price up until buyers stop responding, then down until sellers stop responding. Each of those probes is an auction. Everything else follows from watching how the auction behaves.

### Price, time, value

- **Price** advertises an opportunity.
- **Time** regulates it. A price that is wrong gets very little time.
- **Volume** confirms it. Trade either builds there or it does not.

A price that gets time and volume has been **accepted**. That is value. A price that is touched and left has been **rejected**. Price moves ahead of value all the time. The question on every move is whether value follows.

### Who is trading

| Group | Horizon | Behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Day timeframe** | Inside the session | Scalpers, market makers, day traders. They provide liquidity and rotate price. They do not move value. |
| **Other timeframe (OTF)** | Days to months | Funds, position traders, hedgers. They buy what they see as cheap and sell what they see as dear. They are the ones who move value. |

The whole method comes down to one job: work out what the other-timeframe participant is doing, and how confident they are.

### Initiative versus responsive

Activity is classified against the previous session's value area.

| Activity | Where it happens | Type | Tends to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buying | Below prior value | Responsive | Restore balance. Price was cheap, buyers reacted. |
| Buying | Inside or above prior value | Initiative | Continue. Buyers are paying up. |
| Selling | Above prior value | Responsive | Restore balance. Price was dear, sellers reacted. |
| Selling | Inside or below prior value | Initiative | Continue. Sellers are hitting lower prices. |

Responsive activity is expected and usually fades. Initiative activity is conviction and is the kind that starts trends.

### Balance and imbalance

Markets spend most of their time balancing inside a range, and a smaller share of the time moving from one range to the next. The correct behaviour flips between the two:

- **In balance:** the edges are where the opportunity is. Fade them back toward the middle.
- **In imbalance:** go with the move. Fading it is the most expensive mistake in the method.

### The case library in these terms

- **Nov 8:** price lost the POC and sold off from inside value. Initiative selling.
- **Nov 10, the low:** buying far below value after a flush. Responsive buying, which is why the first target was a return to value, not a new trend.
- **Nov 4, the break of 78.6k:** buying above value. Initiative, and it ran.

### Check yourself

<details>
<summary>Price opens above yesterday's value area and sellers push it back inside. Initiative or responsive?</summary>

Responsive selling. Price was above value and sellers reacted to it. Expect it to work toward balance unless the selling carries on down through value, at which point it has become initiative.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why does the same tactic that works on Monday lose money on Tuesday?</summary>

Because Monday was balance and Tuesday was imbalance. Fading edges is right in one and wrong in the other. Identify which regime you are in before choosing a tactic.

</details>
