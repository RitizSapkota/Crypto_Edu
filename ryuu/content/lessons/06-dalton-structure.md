### The pieces

- **Initial balance (IB).** The range of the first hour, which is the first two 30-minute periods. It is set mostly by short-term traders finding a range to work in. A wide IB tends to contain the day. A narrow IB is easy to break.
- **Range extension.** Any trade beyond the IB after the first hour. It means a longer-timeframe participant has stepped in on that side.
- **Tails.** Single blocks at an extreme, at least two rows deep to count. The market tried a price and was thrown back fast. A longer tail means a stronger rejection. Dalton calls this excess, and it marks the end of an auction in that direction.
- **Poor high or poor low.** An extreme with no tail, where several periods stopped at the same price. The auction did not finish. These levels tend to be revisited.
- **Ledge.** A flat shelf inside the profile where price stopped repeatedly.
- **Spike.** A fast move away from value late in the session, too late for time to confirm it.

Dalton also counts the TPOs above and below the POC on rotational days as a rough gauge of which side is more active inside value. The method is in the books.

### Day types

You cannot know the day type at the open. You recognise it as it forms, and the earlier you do, the better your tactics fit.

| Day type | Initial balance | What happens | How to trade it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Normal** | Wide | The first hour sets the range and little happens outside it. Fairly rare. | Fade the edges of value. |
| **Normal variation** | Moderate | One side extends the range, typically by up to the size of the IB. The most common type. | Go with the extension once it is accepted. The other IB edge tends to hold. |
| **Trend** | Narrow | Price leaves the open and keeps going, with shallow pullbacks. A tall thin profile that closes near its extreme. | Do not fade. Join on pullbacks and hold. |
| **Double distribution trend** | Narrow, quiet | A quiet first distribution, then a decisive move to a new area where a second one forms. Single prints separate them. | Trade it as a trend. The single prints between the two act as support or resistance. |
| **Neutral** | Moderate | Range extension on both sides. Both longer-timeframe sides are active and neither wins. | Closing near the middle means indecision: fade. Closing at an extreme means one side won late: lean that way next session. |
| **Non-trend** | Narrow | A tiny range with no conviction, often ahead of news. | Little to do. A break of the tight range can be significant. |

### The two expensive mistakes

1. Fading a trend day because price looks stretched.
2. Chasing breakouts on a rotational day and getting chopped at both edges.

### Late spikes

A spike late in a session has not had time to prove itself. The next session's open settles it. Opening and holding within the spike accepts it. Opening beyond it in the same direction confirms it strongly. Opening back below the base of an up spike, or above the base of a down spike, rejects it.

### Check yourself

<details>
<summary>The first hour is very narrow. Two hours later price breaks above it and holds. What are you looking at, and what should you not do?</summary>

A narrow initial balance with accepted range extension, which is how trend days and double distributions start. Do not short it because it looks extended.

</details>

<details>
<summary>A session high has three periods stopping at the same tick with no tail. What does that suggest?</summary>

A poor high. Sellers did not reject it with force, so the auction up is unfinished and the level is likely to be tested again.

</details>
