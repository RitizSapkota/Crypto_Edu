### Four states

| State | What you see | Meaning | In the case library |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Conviction** | Frequent large market orders, mostly one side. | Someone wants in and is paying for it. | Nov 10, 20:10: the first real buy delta of the rally. |
| **Momentum** | Market orders keep overwhelming the resting orders. | The book cannot hold price. Do not stand in front of it. | Nov 5, 14:45: bids eaten level after level. |
| **Absorption** | Frequent large market orders that fail to move price. | A passive participant is taking the other side. | Nov 10 low: heavy selling into bids at 73k that held. |
| **Exhaustion** | Market orders get smaller and rarer as price extends. | The aggressor is running out. | Nov 10, the lower low at 72.7k on much smaller delta. |

Always separate the aggression from its outcome. Delta tells you who was hitting. Price tells you whether it worked.

### What a footprint shows

A footprint bar splits a candle into price rows. Each row shows volume traded at the bid and at the ask, so you can see where inside the bar the aggression happened and where it was met. The bar statistics underneath break delta down by trade size.

### Only the anomalies matter

A good rule for footprint: the only thing that matters is anomalies, and they live at the tails of the bars, not in the middle. The middle of a bar is noise. An extreme reading at the top or bottom of a bar is information.

### Wait for dominance to shift

A typical trap is a rally that prints a bar with strong selling at the top. It looks like a short. If you are scalping momentum, wait until dominance has fully shifted, or you get trapped. That first bar often traps people.

The method is to wait for the next rotation. A new bar opens, the delta from the anomaly gets taken back and flipped, the traders caught on the wrong side exit, and you ride that. If it does not happen, you at least know price is at a possible exhaustion point.

### Three ways it resolves

- **Quick and explosive.** The shift confirms and price runs.
- **A fade.** The next rotation leaves the anomaly behind with an extreme the other way. Less common.
- **A slow bleed.** The opposing extreme stops the move and exhausts it, but nothing takes over. Price drifts for hours. The top is still the top.

### Aggregated flow tools

Some platforms combine spot and derivatives flow into one coloured trend reading. The short version of how traders use them: price goes up while the reading is buy-side, and when it flips hard to sell-side the move is usually over. Most of these values only fix when the bar closes, so an intrabar reading can still change.

### Check yourself

<details>
<summary>A bar at the high shows the largest sell delta of the day in its top rows. Do you short it?</summary>

Not yet. One bar is an anomaly, not a shift. Wait for the next rotation to open and for delta to flip and hold. If it does, the trapped buyers are your fuel. If it does not, you have saved a loss.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Sell delta is large and steady, but price has not made a new low for an hour. Which state?</summary>

Absorption. Aggressive sellers are being filled by passive buyers. It becomes a long idea once price starts to lift while the selling continues or fades.

</details>
