### Definition details

- A single print is only confirmed after the following period closes without trading back into it.
- Single blocks at the very top or bottom of a profile are **tails**, not single prints. Tails are rejection of an extreme.
- When a move leaves a run of single prints, the one that matters most is the first one from the breakaway: the lowest in a rally, the highest in a decline.

### The order flow read: they get filled

Single prints are left when the market is too exuberant. Price comes back and fills them most of the time. There is no deep theory behind it; it is an observation from screen time. Two refinements:

- **Session prints are more reliable than daily ones.** A print left inside the Asia, London or New York session profile is a better target than one on the full-day profile.
- **A fill can fail.** A typical case: a second print window does not fill. The print had been created by stops triggering as price slid through a large volume area and its composite POC, and the buyback from that POC was too strong. Expect another attempt, with a mechanical bounce after the fill.

### Dalton's read: they hold while the move is real

In the Market Profile books, single prints in the body of a profile separate two distributions. They mark the moment a longer-timeframe participant moved price with conviction. While those prints stay untouched, the side that made them is in control. If price trades back through them, the conviction is gone.

### Putting both together

| Situation | Expect | Use the prints as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same session, move still fresh, flow still one-sided | Prints hold | Support or resistance. Trading through them is your warning. |
| Later sessions, market has gone back to balance | Prints attract price and fill | A target. Expect a reaction at the first print once it fills. |
| Print sits beyond a major composite POC | Fill may fail on the first try | A target only after the POC reaction has played out. |

Neither source gives a fill rate. "Most of the time" is an observation, not a statistic. Count it yourself.

### Weekend context

A gap or print made on a weekend sits in thin trade. A widely held view is that price tends to revert toward the mean on Monday, which makes the case for filling a weekend print stronger.

### Check yourself

<details>
<summary>The London session drives up and leaves single prints. An hour later price drops back through all of them. What changed?</summary>

The buyers who drove the move did not defend it. By Dalton's read, control has shifted or was never there. By the order flow read, the gap has simply been filled. Either way the prints are no longer support.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is a long lower wick on the day's profile a single print?</summary>

No. Single blocks at the extreme are a tail. They show rejection of that extreme and follow different logic.

</details>
